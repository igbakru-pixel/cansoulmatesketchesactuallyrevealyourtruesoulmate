<h2><strong>Why They Can Feel Accurate: The Barnum Effect</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The main reason readings feel personal is the Barnum effect, also called the Forer effect. The Cleveland Clinic describes the</span><a href="https://health.clevelandclinic.org/barnum-effect"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Barnum effect</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> as the tendency to accept really general or vague statements as if they were unique to us.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is stronger when you believe the information was personalized for you and you apply your own meaning to it.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Psychology Today adds that the effect grows when predictions are positive, carry personalized labels or come from someone who seems to have authority, and that people can protect themselves by watching for vague messages.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lines like "you have loved deeply and been hurt" or "someone from your past still thinks of you" fit nearly everyone.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A psychologist quoted in coverage of the effect puts it simply: self-validation is no validation.</span></p>
